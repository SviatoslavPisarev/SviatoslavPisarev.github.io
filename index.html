01 / About

Chemistry, from crystals to cells

I'm a Ph.D. student in Chemistry at the CUNY Graduate Center. My interests sit where nanotechnology meets medicinal chemistry, and I like attacking problems from both sides: making and measuring materials at the bench, and modeling them on the computer.

I started in solid-state chemistry, synthesizing rare-earth intermetallics by arc-melting and solving their structures from powder diffraction. A summer at the CUNY Advanced Science Research Center moved me toward nanoscale materials, studying how polymers thread through metal–organic frameworks.

Today, in Dr. Hiroshi Matsui's lab, I study how engineered nanomaterials, especially iron oxide nanocages, interact with biological systems, with the goal of new tools for cancer research and treatment.

02 / Research

Research journey

Three labs, three length scales: atoms in a crystal, polymers in a pore, nanoparticles meeting cells.

Nanotechnology and cancer research Current

Aug 2026 – present

Graduate Researcher · Matsui Lab, Department of Chemistry, Hunter College · thematsuilab.github.io

Investigating nanomaterials and their interactions with biological systems, with a focus on advancing medical applications.

Magnetic remodeling of the tumor matrix

Using iron oxide nanocages driven by an alternating magnetic field to rearrange collagen in hydrogels, imaged by confocal reflectance microscopy.

Modeling functionalized nanocages

Building atomistic models of catechol-coated iron oxide nanocages and setting up molecular dynamics and docking to study drug and DNA binding.

Immune–cancer cell assays

3D viability assays on MDA-MB-231 triple-negative breast cancer cells, testing treatments that may boost T-cell killing.

Polyethylene glycol threading through ZIF-8

Jun – Aug 2025

Velay Summer Undergraduate Research Fellow · Nanoscience Initiative, CUNY Advanced Science Research Center · Dr. Christopher DelRe

  • Studied polymer–MOF composites: how polyethylene glycol (PEG) infiltrates the metal–organic framework ZIF-8.
  • Synthesized ZIF-8 and characterized the nanoparticles by dynamic light scattering.
  • Helped analyze polymer–framework interactions by solution NMR (including PULCON), MALDI, PXRD and DSC.
  • Presented the work orally at the Velay Summer Undergraduate Research Conference.

Solid-state intermetallics for rare-earth separation

May 2024 – Dec 2025

Undergraduate Researcher · Department of Chemistry, Hunter College · Dr. Anton Oliynyk

  • Expanded the RERE'Ir2Ge4 series with 43 new phases, where two different rare-earth metals sit on separate crystallographic sites.
  • Prepared samples by pressing pellets, arc-melting, sealing and annealing.
  • Identified phases and impurities with powder X-ray diffraction and SEM; refined unit cells with Pawley and Rietveld methods.
  • Wrote Python code to compare unit-cell parameters across compounds that contain two rare-earth metals.
